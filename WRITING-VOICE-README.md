# Writing voice for this portfolio

This is a reference for future non-technical copy: homepage text, About text, section introductions, project summaries, and Recent Updates cards.

The goal is not to make the site sound more casual or friendly. The goal is to make it sound like me.

## Basic rules

### Say what I actually do

Prefer direct descriptions:

- "I work in cybersecurity and IT."
- "My background is mostly systems and networking."
- "I currently work as a Security Analyst in an MSP environment."
- "Most of my recent personal time has been focused on penetration testing."

Avoid turning the same facts into branding language such as "operator," "security practitioner," "proven capability," "strong signal," or similar phrasing I would not normally use.

### Keep current experience separate from where I am headed

Use different language for different levels of experience:

- **Current work:** "I work...", "I built...", "I found..."
- **Current focus:** "I am working on...", "I am trying to improve..."
- **Newer interests:** "I am exploring...", "I am spending more time on..."

Do not make DFIR, threat intelligence, OSINT, threat hunting, or another newer interest sound like an established specialty unless that actually changes later.

### Do not hide the mixed IT/security background

The systems, networking, support, and Army technical background is part of the story. The portfolio does not need to pretend every job or project was pure cybersecurity.

### First person is fine

For personal and summary writing, use "I". This is my portfolio, not a consulting report.

Technical writeups can still become more formal where the subject calls for it, but introductions and summaries should not sound detached just for the sake of sounding professional.

### Be specific instead of impressive

Prefer:

> I built a dedicated OPNsense firewall on repurposed Dell hardware.

Over:

> Designed and implemented a robust next-generation network security platform.

Prefer:

> I passed CPTS and want to improve my methodology, enumeration, reporting, and Active Directory skills.

Over:

> CPTS validates advanced offensive-security capability across complex enterprise environments.

### Leave room to grow

The site is a record of work I want to keep and share as I continue to learn. It does not need to make every project sound finished, senior-level, or career-defining.
